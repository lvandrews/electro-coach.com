Electro-Coach privacy

Effective 12 November 2026

The short version. There’s no account. What you enter stays on your device. The app sends a few small, specific requests, listed below, and never sends your shots, scores, notes, routine, profile, names or venues anywhere. The one exception is a few words you choose to write under Other in the players’ survey (see below).

What stays on your device

  • Everything you enter or import: Railbird exports, drill tallies, sessions, games and matches, journal entries, your pre-shot routine, your focus card, your player profile, settings.
  • Backups are files you save and keep yourself. Delete everything at any time with Settings → Delete all data; uninstalling the Android app does the same.
  • Automatic backups (if you turn them on in Settings → Backup): each day, when you open the app and again when you finish a drill or a session, the app saves a backup into the one folder you chose, on your device or in a cloud folder you sync yourself. It goes only there, never to Electro-Coach; the app keeps one file a day, the newest 10 days, and touches nothing else in the folder.
  • The camera (Android app, only when you tap Scan a QR code in Settings → Tournament calendar): Android asks you first. The app reads the camera’s picture on the phone to find the QR code, and turns the camera off once it has read the link, when you tap Stop, or after two minutes. Nothing is recorded, kept or sent.
  • Android’s own backup: if your phone backs up to your Google account, Android may include Electro-Coach’s data in that backup. It goes to your own Google account (encrypted when your phone has a screen lock), never to Electro-Coach. To leave it out, turn off backup in your phone’s settings.

What the app sends, and to whom

  • Anonymous usage counts (Aptabase, a privacy-first analytics service): part of the free version, and the app says so when you first open it; they’ll be optional in the paid version. They show which screens, drills and features get used, how long sets run and the levels reached (in broad ranges), the answers you choose in your player profile to “How do you like to practice?” and “Where do you compete?”, your answer to “Does this match what you or your coach see?” under the Stroke check (Yes, Partly or No, and which kind of story it answered), your answers to the occasional players’ survey (fixed choices, such as “Ghost drills” or “Shot clock”, and, only if you write them, a few words under Other: at most 80 characters, with anything that looks like an email address, phone number or link taken out on your phone before it’s sent; please don’t put your name in them), whether you put the survey off or turned it off, a count when the app hits an error (only where: on a screen, a file that didn’t load, an update while the app was open, or in the background; the error’s standard type, such as TypeError, from a fixed list; what kind of thing failed (an error, an empty one, a browser event, an object, a text or a number); and for the app framework’s own errors, their code from a fixed list, such as UNAVAILABLE; never the error’s text, which could hold your data), and how many weeks it has been since the app was first opened (also a range), with the app version, the language setting and the description every browser gives websites (on Android, the phone’s model and its Android and web view versions). Aptabase works out the country from the connection and doesn’t store the address. No account, device ID or install ID; nothing you enter, except the few words you choose to write under the survey’s Other. The counts are never sold.
  • Update check (Android app, GitHub): each time the app starts, and at most twice a day while it stays open, the app asks GitHub for the latest release. Nothing about you is sent.
  • FargoRate (only if you link your FargoRate): at most one request a day for your rating, sending only your FargoRate ID, plus any search you make. Switch the daily check off on the FargoRate page.
  • Tournament calendar (Android app, only if you link one): the app reads your Google, Outlook or Apple calendar with the private link you paste or scan, when you scan or link it, when you tap Read it now, and when you open the app (at most every 6 hours), and keeps the events’ dates, names and locations on your phone to show your tournaments. The link is only ever sent to the calendar’s own service (Google, Microsoft or Apple), which sees the request as it would from any calendar app. The link, the events, the ones you mark Not going, the game and place you set for them and your answers after a tournament (how it went, where it broke down) are left out of Electro-Coach’s backup files and go nowhere else; Unlink removes them. (Android’s own backup or transfer of your phone, to your Google account or a new phone, can include any app’s storage, this one’s too.) To bring the link from a computer, electro-coach.com/calendar turns it into a QR code inside that page, which sends nothing anywhere and isn’t counted as a visit; the app reads the code with the camera (above).
  • The website and the web version are hosted by GitHub Pages, which keeps standard visitor logs like any website; GitHub’s privacy statement covers those.
  • Website visit counts (electro-coach.com only, Aptabase): each page of the website you open (other than the calendar page) is counted once, with only which page (home, privacy or terms), the website that sent you there (just its name, such as google.com, or “direct”) and your browser’s language. Aptabase works out the country and the kind of device from the connection and doesn’t store the address. No cookies, nothing stored in your browser, no ID, and nothing at all when your browser asks sites not to track you (Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control). The web version of the app isn’t part of these counts; it has the usage counts above.

Things you choose to share

  • Feedback (problems, ideas and drill requests, and a “Something else” note from a film review or stroke check-in when you tap Send to the developer) opens an email to feedback@electro-coach.com in your own mail app, with the app version and the kind of device filled in. Nothing is sent until you press Send. What you write and your email address reach Electro-Coach through Proton Mail; they’re used only to read and answer it and to improve the app, and never shared or sold.
  • Sharing your journal, a summary or a finished match (with a coach, a chatbot, your opponent or by email) happens only when you tap Share. A match file holds the two players’ names (as typed, or your profile name), when it was played, the score, each rack and the shot clock times. After that it’s covered by whoever receives it.
  • Payments. There are none yet. When the paid version arrives, purchases will go through Google Play or a checkout service, which handle the card details; the app never sees them. This page will say what those services receive before then.

Notifications (Android) are created on the phone: practice reminders, the morning recap, FargoRate changes, the end of a timed practice block, and the time left while you film one (a paired watch can show them too). Nothing is sent to produce them.

Children. Electro-Coach isn’t aimed at children under 13 and doesn’t knowingly collect anything from them; it collects nothing personal from anyone.

Changes. If this changes, the new version comes with the app update and is noted in the release notes.
